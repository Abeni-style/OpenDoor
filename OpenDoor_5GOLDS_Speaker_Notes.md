# OpenDoor - Team 5GOLDS speaker notes

## Opening (20 seconds)
Good day, judges. We are Team 5GOLDS, and our venture is OpenDoor. OpenDoor is a global student opportunity and development platform. It helps students discover opportunities, understand published requirements, prepare applications and build practical skills.

## Core pitch (about 2 minutes)
Students often face an information-to-action gap. Opportunities are scattered, eligibility requirements can be difficult to compare, and application preparation requires skills and organization. OpenDoor brings these steps together. Students can explore scholarships, programs, internships, competitions and research opportunities; compare basic profile details with known listing criteria; develop skills through structured lessons; and organize applications using practical templates and a local planner.

The product is designed for students worldwide. Our early validation focus is Ethiopia, where the team can reach students and educators for feedback before expanding.

Our current prototype includes an opportunity explorer, rules-based matcher, 12 Academy courses, 36 lessons, 24 knowledge-check questions, application-preparation templates and browser-local tracking tools. The starter catalogue contains 52 discovery leads and 61 source-register entries. These are not all confirmed open opportunities: 51 entries are pending review, one GVL 2026 entry is marked closed, and current age/country/funding/deadline fields need official verification.

The business model is freemium. Core opportunity discovery and essential information remain free. Premium may later add convenience features, advanced planning and cloud services. Institutional licensing is another possible revenue stream. Payments are not active and will remain disabled until appropriate adult-controlled billing and legal safeguards are in place.

The front-end prototype is built. Backend source has been prepared for Supabase, a server-side AI assistant, moderated feedback, hosted subscription checkout and scheduled feed collection. Those services have not yet been deployed or activated. Our next steps are student validation, source verification, controlled deployment and a small school/community pilot.

## Judge Q&A
**Why not just use Google?** Google can find pages, but OpenDoor structures opportunity records around student-relevant fields and connects discovery to matching, preparation and tracking. We do not claim to replace official providers or every existing platform.

**Is every opportunity verified?** No. Our seed directory has 52 discovery leads. They are not all current or verified. The product is designed to make that status visible and to require human verification before publication.

**Is the AI live?** The server-side integration code is prepared, but the provider key and Supabase deployment are not configured. The matching demonstration in this prototype is rules-based, not a deployed AI model.

**How do you make money?** Essential discovery stays free. A future Premium tier may charge for extra convenience and advanced tools. Institutional subscriptions are another possibility. Payment is not active.

**What is your biggest risk?** Maintaining accurate, current opportunity information at scale. We address it through official sources, verification timestamps, a pending-review workflow and a narrower initial pilot.

**What will you do first?** Interview 20-30 students and 3-5 educators, verify a smaller catalogue, test the product with real users and measure whether it helps them identify appropriate opportunities and complete next steps.

**What do you need from the virtual investment simulation?** We would prioritize product engineering, opportunity verification, student testing/partnerships and privacy/security. The allocation on slide 14 is illustrative and can be adjusted to the organizers’ investment rules.
